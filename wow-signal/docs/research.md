# Research notes: the Wow! signal

Every claim in the script, and its source.

| line | fact | source |
|---|---|---|
| "August, 1977. A radio telescope in Ohio" | Detected 15 Aug 1977 by Ohio State University's Big Ear radio telescope (Delaware, Ohio), part of a SETI search running since 1973 | Wikipedia "Wow! signal"; Ehman, *The Big Ear Wow! Signal: 30th anniversary report* (bigear.org) |
| 11:16 PM (on screen) | "around 22:16:34 EST (about 10:16 p.m. EST or 11:16 p.m. EDT)"; Ohio was on EDT in August | Ehman 30th-anniversary report; Wikipedia gives 22:16 EST (23:16 EDT) |
| "the computer runs out of numbers, and switches to letters" | intensity printed as one character: blank for 0, digits 1-9, then "the integer values from 10 through 35 were printed out as the upper case letters A through Z" | Ehman report |
| 6 E Q U J 5 = 6, 14, 26, 30, 19, 5 | intensity over time in multiples of the background noise; U = 30-31 | Wikipedia; Ehman: "30.5 +/- 0.5 times stronger than the background noise" |
| "30X stronger than the noise" (on screen) | as above | Ehman report |
| "seventy-two seconds, exactly like a signal from deep space" | the signal lasted the full 72-second window Big Ear could watch a point; rise and fall matched a celestial source drifting through the beam | Wikipedia; Ehman |
| "the exact frequency scientists had guessed aliens would use" | near 1420 MHz, the hydrogen line, which Cocconi & Morrison (1959) speculated an extraterrestrial civilisation might use | Wikipedia |
| channel 2 (CRT label, printout column) | 6EQUJ5 appears in channel 2 of the 50-channel receiver | Wikipedia (printout description) |
| "Days later, astronomer Jerry Ehman finds it. He circles it in red, and writes one word." | found "a few days after... (probably on August 19)" reviewing printouts; "In the red pen I was using I immediately highlighted those six characters and wrote the notation 'Wow!'"; Wikipedia: "circled" | Ehman report; Wikipedia |
| "Telescopes have searched that spot for decades. It never came back." | searches by Ehman (1977), Gray (META 1987, 1989; VLA 1995-96), Shuch (1995), Gray & Ellingsen (1999), Breakthrough Listen (2022): nothing | Wikipedia |
| "Torn down in 1998... to expand a golf course." | "destroyed in 1998 by land developers" who bought the land "to convert a 9-hole golf course into an 18-hole course" and build ~400 houses | Ehman report; bigear.org; Ohio historical marker |
| "In 2024, scientists suggested a natural flash, in a cloud of hydrogen. But nobody can prove it." | Méndez, Ortiz Ceballos & Zuluaga, *Arecibo Wow! I* (arXiv:2408.08513, Aug 2024): a transient source such as a magnetar flare brightening a cold hydrogen cloud; authors call it a hypothesis | arXiv; Scientific American; Science News |

Deliberately not claimed: that it was aliens (the hook says "might be"), that nobody was at the
observatory that night, where Ehman was sitting, or any distance to the source.
